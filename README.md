### 🥈 RoboCup Singapore Soccer Open 2025
**First Runner-Up**

I competed in the RoboCup Junior Robot Soccer category using an **ESP32** as the main controller.

The robot used three omni wheels positioned 120° apart, allowing it to move in different directions without rotating.

For vision, I used:

- **OpenMV + conical mirror** for wide-angle vision
- **Pixy2** for front-facing ball tracking

PID-based control was used to improve the robot's movement toward the ball.

After our initial performance was below expectations, we iteratively modified the software during the competition, significantly improving the robot's performance.

**Technologies:**  
`ESP32` `OpenMV` `Pixy2` `PID Control` `Computer Vision` `Fusion 360`

### 🥇 TPA Robo Rescue with micro-ROS 2025
**First Place**

An autonomous robotics competition focused on obstacle avoidance, navigation, and survivor search.

The 2025 competition introduced **micro-ROS**, requiring teams to work with a new robotics software architecture.

Before the competition, I independently studied micro-ROS and its communication framework to prepare for the qualification process.

During development, our robot experienced inconsistent LiDAR measurements that caused localization errors and unstable navigation.

I investigated the localization system and tuned the **AMCL parameters**, significantly improving localization accuracy and map stability.

Our robot was then able to navigate reliably and complete all tasks.

Our team achieved a **perfect score in three consecutive rounds**, becoming the only team to achieve full scores in all three rounds.

### 🥇 Silapa 71 Intermediate Robotics
**Gold Medal**

This competition focused on light sensors and consisted of two rounds: a standard track and a maze.

For the standard round, teams were given a limited amount of time to prepare for a known track. I developed a modular control system that allowed me to quickly implement different behaviors for each section of the track.

The maze round required the robot to navigate an unknown environment autonomously.

I implemented a block-based exploration algorithm. At each position, the robot checked available paths and stored branching positions using an array.

The robot continued exploring until it reached a dead end, then returned to previously stored positions and explored alternative paths.

This allowed the robot to autonomously explore an unknown maze.

**Technologies:**  
`Light Sensors` `Path Exploration` `Arrays` `Embedded Programming`
